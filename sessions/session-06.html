Sessions 11–12

Structured Thinking over Tool Tricks: The Perplexity Case

Nov 1, 2026Day 6Perplexity case

Why the order of analysis matters: assets first, then niche or general, then segment, and only then features.

Intended learning outcome

Sequence frameworks correctly in a strategy case (complementary assets, then niche versus general purpose, then segmentation, targeting and positioning), and use AI tools to surface frameworks without replacing the thinking.

Executive Summary

  • Using AI to draft and improve a prompt, then reading the revised prompt line by line, beats copying it blindly
  • The professor restated the course aim: structured thinking for business decisions, with AI as the accelerant rather than the subject
  • On the Perplexity case, class answers jumped straight to features; the correct route is Teece, then niche, then STP, then features

Live demonstration: from prompt to published page

  1. 1Draft the prompt — in one AI tool, describing the case discussion and the frameworks used
  2. 2Improve the prompt — by asking the tool to refine it, then read the revised version line by line before running it
  3. 3Generate the page — outside the hosting platform; upload only the finished files, since platform-side AI use consumes credits
  4. 4Work around plan limits — by splitting the work across tools when a free plan caps attachments
  5. 5Check what already exists — by verifying earlier session tabs remained intact after the update

The professor noted that a multi-model interface lets you try several models from one place, and mentioned a separate tool for operational data analysis as an independent-study option outside the syllabus.

The Perplexity case

  • Thesis — an advertising model can compromise search quality, so build an answering engine that prioritises accuracy and speed over ad clicks
  • Business model — subscription-led, aimed at professionals, researchers, analysts and senior leaders rather than the mass market
  • Valuation — grew quickly to a peak in early 2026, then flattened
  • Reformulator — a layer that reinterprets and clarifies the user’s prompt, reducing dependence on precise prompt engineering
  • Index — a weighted database that ranks credible sources above weaker ones
  • RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) — retrieve relevant indexed content, add it to the model’s context, then generate a cited answer; this improves accuracy and reduces hallucination
  • Feature options — Deep Search, an Enterprise version, and Voice Search

What the class recommended first

Suggestions covered Deep Search with multi-step retrieval, an enterprise push with API partnerships, a Scholar-style research platform with verified sources, an agentic browser play, and more distribution partnerships. Each was reasonable. The professor’s point was that they were opinions looking for justification.

The framework-based derivation

  1. 1Complementary assets (Teece) — Perplexity lacks the cloud infrastructure that Microsoft, Google and Amazon bring to their own models, so competing head-on as a general-purpose tool is untenable
  2. 2Niche versus general purpose — the framework conclusion is a niche, specific-purpose product
  3. 3STP: segmentation, targeting, positioning — only now choose segments such as researchers, financial analysts or medical professionals, then pick the feature (deep search, enterprise or voice) that serves that segment best

The professor distinguished deriving a conclusion through structured reasoning from justifying a conclusion already formed. A custom GPT suggested Blue Ocean Strategy as another lens, which showed the right use of AI: identify applicable frameworks, then do the thinking.

Where the course is heading

Part of the session was a candid discussion on direction. Students asked for more business-decision content, such as choosing tools for specific decisions and judging AI investment return, more collaborative work, and cases drawn from their own workplaces and from India. Governance questions were also raised, including licence rollout, cost per prompt and scaling a proof of concept into business value.

  • Aim restated — MBA education trains structured thinking for decisions; AI accelerates it
  • Tools versus frameworks — frameworks such as Teece, STP and Blue Ocean drive decisions; AI tools speed up finding and applying them
  • Mindset shift — technically trained students lean toward single-answer thinking; the MBA aims to build multi-path, judgement-based reasoning
  • Response — the course stays within its structure, absorbing suggestions such as real workplace problems per class and real corporate AI transformation cases, with changes coordinated through the class coordinator
  • Foundation first — this is a compulsory foundation course; specialised AI electives will go deeper

Open questions

  • Which frameworks should anchor the Perplexity conclusion: Blue Ocean, STP or Teece?
  • Will the Perplexity case be completed next session, or replaced by a student-chosen business problem?
  • How will the class serve different technical levels if content moves to advanced business integration?

Leadership Reflection

What this session means for manufacturing leaders:

Sequence matters. In a plant, choosing an AI tool before defining the decision is the same error as choosing a feature before the framework. Ask in order: what assets do we hold, which niche can we win, who is the customer, and only then which tool.

The feedback discussion carries a lesson too. The most useful AI work starts from a real operational problem, and scaling from pilot to value is decided by governance, ownership and cost, not by the model.
