Working with ChatGPT: The AI Wars Case

Sep 12, 2026Day 2HBS Case 9-723-434"AI Wars"

Why Google, despite superior AI research, lost first-mover advantage to OpenAI — and what that means for how large organisations compete on AI.

Executive Summary

  • The AI Wars case examines competitive dynamics between Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, and Anthropic as of February 2024
  • Google's strategic dilemma: superior AI research — including its own 2017 "Attention Is All You Need" paper — wasn't enough to beat OpenAI to market
  • Large tech companies increasingly collaborate with AI startups rather than build everything in-house; organisational size can slow innovation
  • The open-source vs. closed-source debate reflects fundamentally different strategic bets — Meta's Llama vs. OpenAI's closed models
  • AI governance emerged as the single biggest competency gap in organisations, per an executive survey conducted in a prior session

Discussion Highlights

  1. 1Background & context — ChatGPT's Nov 2022 release triggered a "code red" at Google; the case's four components are technical competency, implementation, governance, and strategy
  2. 2Why Google wasn't first — a mix of fear of cannibalising search-ad revenue, reputational risk as a large incumbent, and internal ethical debate, including Geoffrey Hinton's 2023 resignation
  3. 3Strategic collaborations — Microsoft–OpenAI ($1B → $10B investment, 49% profit share), Google/Amazon–Anthropic; big-company resources plus startup agility as the emerging winning formula
  4. 4Open vs. closed source — Meta's ad-driven model favours open Llama; OpenAI's product-driven model favours closed; the real strategic question is what to open, not whether
  5. 5AI business models — five main revenue categories: chatbots, search, productivity software, APIs, cloud; Google's ad economics are directly threatened if AI answers replace clicked links
  6. 6Existential threat & disruption — parallels drawn to Kodak and Yahoo; a shift in consumer search habits is flagged as the biggest long-term risk to Google
  7. 7Governance structures — Anthropic's five-person Long-Term Benefit Trust and OpenAI's non-profit/for-profit split show AI companies adopting unprecedented oversight layers
  8. 8AI in business education — technical AI competency is now foundational, not elective; governance shows the largest importance-vs-preparedness gap

Leadership Reflection

What this session means for manufacturing leaders:

The AI Wars case reinforced that technological leadership alone does not guarantee market leadership.

For manufacturing businesses, the key lesson is that successful AI transformation depends less on model sophistication and more on speed, adoption, governance, ecosystem strategy, and the ability to redesign operating models around new capabilities.

The challenge for future leaders is not identifying the best AI model, but deciding where AI changes customer value, decision-making, and competitive advantage.
