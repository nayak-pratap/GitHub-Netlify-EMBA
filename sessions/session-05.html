Sessions 9–10

Innovation Frameworks and the Google Gemini Case

Oct 25, 2026Day 5Frameworks

Why did Google, which invented the transformer, not win the first-mover race in Gen AI, and what do two innovation frameworks say about what happens next?

Intended learning outcome

Apply two innovation frameworks to a live competitive case, structure an AI strategy in two directions, and derive recommendations step by step instead of answering from intuition.

Executive Summary

  • Google invented the technology yet ChatGPT captured the early lead, because of slow big-company decisions, a weak early launch, early hallucinations and premium pricing
  • Frameworks showed Google still holds strong complementary assets: cloud, custom chips and a wide ecosystem
  • AI strategy must run in two directions, bottom-up improvement and top-down reinvention
  • “Framework first, answer second”: most class answers were impulsive and memory-based, and the professor asked for derivation

Two-directional AI strategy

  • Bottom-up — find where AI improves existing offerings and processes. Necessary but not sufficient
  • Top-down — the CXO question of whether AI could reinvent the business model or make it obsolete
  • Mindset lock-in — focusing only on micro-improvements, through offering lock-in or process lock-in
  • Threat lens — assess threats to core assets and to core activities. If both are threatened, the logic is “harvest and exit”

A classmate from a commercial-vehicle manufacturer described competitors launching new models about twice as fast. The team’s AI focus is virtual verification, replacing physical prototype testing with end-to-end virtual design checks to compress development time. That is value-chain thinking, not a process tweak. Another classmate noted that in organisations still in transition, frontline staff often see AI leverage points before management does, which supports a bi-directional approach.

The professor suggested treating the project as a potential research paper rather than a routine assignment.

The Gemini case

  • The paradox — Google published the transformer architecture behind modern Gen AI, yet ChatGPT led. At the time of the case ChatGPT had about 1 billion users against about 300 million for Gemini. By 2026 the gap had narrowed considerably
  • Curse of being big — large organisations move slowly while start-ups iterate fast
  • Early missteps — a failed Bard launch hurt credibility, and early Gemini versions were known for hallucinations
  • Pricing — Gemini sat among the costliest subscriptions without being the market leader
  • Commoditisation — major models perform similarly on the same benchmarks, with few real differentiators

Framework 1: Complementary Asset Framework (Teece)

Innovators rarely capture the value of their own innovation; value flows to whoever controls the complementary assets. For Gen AI the key complementary asset is cloud computing. OpenAI closed its gap through a partnership with Microsoft in exchange for cloud access. Google already owns cloud, custom AI chips and advanced quantum research, a stronger asset position than any rival. The historical parallel is web search: Yahoo moved first, but Google won as second mover with a better product. Google’s bottleneck is therefore product design and execution, not assets.

Framework 2: Pattern of Industrial Innovation (Abernathy and Utterback, 1978)

Products evolve rapidly at first, settle around a dominant design, and then innovation shifts to process. Gen AI is still in the fluid stage: features change every six months and no dominant design has emerged. The light bulb, from carbon filament to tungsten, fluorescent and LED, shows that each stage was won by whoever tracked changing needs fastest. AI and society co-evolve, so the winner is whoever tracks that feedback loop most efficiently. Agentic AI fits as the next stage along the same trajectory, and demand for explainable AI is another example of user needs steering product direction.

Derivation, not impulse

  1. 1State the problem — in one clear sentence
  2. 2List the assumptions — that the analysis rests on
  3. 3Apply the framework logic — step by step
  4. 4Derive the conclusion — that follows from the steps

A price cut, for example, is unjustified until you analyse the value delivered. The cheapest model was used by almost no one in a class of about 70, which shows that price is relative to perceived value. Frameworks can be found in 30 to 60 minutes of research, so skipping them has no excuse.

Exercise: build a framework-recommender tool

Before the next session, every student builds a ChatGPT Plugin or Gemini Skill that takes a business problem as input and returns the most suitable management framework. It recommends a framework; it does not solve the problem.

  • Output — the framework name, a short reason it applies, and two or three guiding questions for applying it
  • Context design — role definition, a framework knowledge base, decision logic for mapping problems to frameworks, output format, and guardrails such as never giving a direct solution or defaulting to one framework
  • Minimum frameworks — Complementary Assets, Pattern of Industrial Innovation, Two-Directional AI Strategy, and the Core Asset and Core Activity threat lens, plus others found through research
  • Review — the next session opens with live demos, the context design, the mapping logic, and limitations

Open questions

  • Does bundling dilute focused product improvement, or does it strengthen the ecosystem advantage?
  • Could quantum computing reshuffle complementary assets and open a new first-mover moment?
  • Can a large company overcome the curse of being big and ship at start-up speed?
  • What monetisation model for Gemini avoids cannibalising core advertising revenue?

Leadership Reflection

What this session means for manufacturing leaders:

Owning the invention is not owning the value. In manufacturing, the winner is often the firm that owns distribution, service networks or data pipelines. Before any AI investment, map which complementary assets you hold and which you would have to borrow through partnerships.

Use the two-directional lens in every transformation: let the plant improve processes from the bottom, while the CXO asks whether a rival could make our product advantage irrelevant. And practise the discipline of derivation. State the problem, list assumptions, apply a framework, then decide.
