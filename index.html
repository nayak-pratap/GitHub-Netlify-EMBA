Transformation Thesis

Manufacturing has spent decades digitizing machines, equipment, and processes.

The next transformation is organizational knowledge.

My working thesis throughout this MBA is that AI will create value not by replacing expertise, but by making expertise scalable.

The organizations that succeed will not simply deploy AI tools. They will redesign how knowledge, decisions, and capabilities flow across the business.

This journal documents how that thinking evolves through the Executive MBA journey.

Key Questions I'm Exploring

How do organizations scale expertise without scaling headcount?
What separates successful AI transformation from isolated AI pilots?
How should manufacturing leaders redesign operating models for AI?
What capabilities will define the next generation of business leaders?
How can AI amplify engineering expertise rather than replace it?

Why IIM Sirmaur

My diploma in Tool & Die Making was about 70% hands-on: die and mould making, CNC machining, CAD/CAM. I learned machining with my hands long before I had words for why it worked.

Years later my B.Tech capstone gave me a way to write it down. It was a design for a Microsoft 365 knowledge management system for cutting tool engineering, and I went on to build the real version at Kennametal, with a Copilot AI agent on top. Nonaka calls this turning tacit knowledge into explicit knowledge.1 I just knew it as not wanting good engineers to take their answers home with them.

I came to IIM Sirmaur because I have already transformed one engineering team. My goal now is to understand how transformation happens at the level of strategy, operating models, leadership, and enterprise capability.

Where This Is Headed

I'm specialising in Business Transformation. Teece describes three things a company needs when the ground shifts under it: noticing the change, acting on it, and reshaping itself around it.2

At Kennametal I've done the middle part. I saw a gap and built something that filled it. What I want from these two years is the first and the last: seeing early where AI will change a market, and knowing how to redesign an organisation around it. My dream is to lead that kind of change in a manufacturing business, and to carry what I learned on the shop floor all the way into the boardroom.

My aspiration is to become a leader who can connect shop-floor reality with boardroom decision-making — combining engineering credibility, business strategy, and AI-enabled transformation.

Notes

  1. Nonaka, I. (1994). A Dynamic Theory of Organizational Knowledge Creation. Organization Science, 5(1) — the SECI model of tacit-to-explicit knowledge conversion.
  2. Teece, D. J. (1997). Dynamic Capabilities and Strategic Management. Strategic Management Journal, 18(7) — the sense–seize–transform framework.
